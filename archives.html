Sol System

Moons and planets in our own system. Each species has a light and a dark card.

Ventkin

Home world Europa, a moon of Jupiter

Benthomnemon europae (proposed)

Under Europa’s ice is a salty ocean that may hold about twice as much water as all of Earth’s oceans. The Ventkin are clear, jelly-soft beings who remember where the warm vents open, and they trade sealed brine because they cannot leave the water.

Icecutters

Home world Enceladus, a moon of Saturn

Rimafossor enceladi (proposed)

Geysers at Enceladus’s south pole spray salt and organic molecules from an ocean under the ice, and that spray helps feed one of Saturn’s rings. The Icecutters are stocky engineers in the tiger-stripe cracks. They cut mineral-rich ice from the walls and trade only when the geysers fire.

Hazedrift

Home world Titan, a moon of Saturn

Nebulambulus titanius (proposed)

Titan is the only world besides Earth known to have lakes and seas, and those lakes are liquid methane and ethane under a thick orange haze. Hazedrift are slow, waxy waders who bottle that haze as pigment and can take a whole day to cross a puddle.

About 50 to 60 km above Venus, where the ground is hot enough to melt lead, the air is closer to Earth’s temperature, though the clouds are sulfuric acid. The Veilborn are floating creatures with glassy, acid-proof skin who never touch the ground.

Red Watchers

Home world Mars

Rubrivigil martis (proposed)

Mars is red because its dust is rusty iron, and dust storms there can wrap the whole planet. The Red Watchers are low, armored burrowers who dig toward old ice and wait those storms out underground, keeping a count that does not change.

Slagbacks

Home world Io, a moon of Jupiter

Io is the most volcanic world in the solar system, with about 400 active volcanoes kept hot by Jupiter’s gravity. Slagbacks are small ember-colored crawlers in stone shells who hop between lava vents and sell sulfur cakes that stay hot.

Wellkeepers

Home world Ceres, the dwarf planet in the asteroid belt

Puteocustos cereris (proposed)

Ceres’s bright spots are salt left behind when salty water from underground seeped up and dried. The Wellkeepers are pale, round-backed diggers who sip that brine and sell the rarest drink in the belt: clean water.

Triton orbits Neptune backwards, and Voyager 2 saw geyser plumes there shoot dark dust about 8 km high. The Farfallen are tall, frost-white stilt-walkers who only move when those geysers go off, trading nitrogen ice before it shrinks.

Lodestars

Home world Ganymede, a moon of Jupiter

OP-S3-80 (model designation)

Ganymede is the biggest moon in the solar system, larger than Mercury, and the only moon known to have its own magnetic field. The Lodestars are robots built on Earth by Earth Exports and sent out ahead of people: tall lantern-headed surveyors, four-legged builders with crane arms, and round greeter drones with open hands. They came first and built the road, and they still build the water-suits the Ventkin need and escort one canister at a time. OP-S3-80 stands for Original Planet, Sol 3 (Earth), 80-year expected service life, and the oldest of them are nowhere near done.

’Oumuamua, spotted in 2017, was the first object seen passing through our solar system from another star. Starwake Comet is a loud crowd under patchwork awnings on a tumbling rock. They sell one mystery crate, then the rock moves on.

Greylight

Home world TRAPPIST-1e

Crepusculus trappistensis (proposed)

TRAPPIST-1e is about Earth’s size, about 40 light-years away, and it likely keeps one face toward its dim red star. Greylight are short, ash-pale counters with wide dark eyes who live in the twilight band between endless day and endless night, and they send tally slips instead of cargo.

Bronzeridge

Home world Kepler-452b

Aeneocrista cygni (proposed)

Kepler-452b was announced as an older cousin of Earth, circling a Sun-like star about once a year, though a later recheck made the planet itself less certain. Bronzeridge are tall, bronze-green officers who inspect every hold and trade seal wax only after the paperwork is right.

Tau Ceti is a nearby Sun-like star with only about a third as much iron as the Sun, wrapped in a dusty belt that likely means more falling rocks. The Ceti Highborn grew up in orbital shelters, weave what they cannot mine, and pay late with a bonus.

Home world Kepler-186f

Calamonumerus ternarius (proposed)

Kepler-186f was the first Earth-size planet found where liquid water might be possible, and midday there would look like the hour before sunset. Darkreed are long, gray-green clerks among dark reeds who count only in threes and buy baskets in sets of 3, 6, or 9.

Proxima Centauri b is the closest known planet outside our solar system, and its small red star throws huge flares. Greenlings are knee-high, moss-green gardeners whose fuzz works as sunscreen, and they dive for cover when a flare is coming.

The Forgotten Men

Home world Barnard's Star (they say; the paperwork is unclear)

Quotidianus barnardi (proposed)

Barnard's Star is a very old red dwarf about 6 light-years away, with a long history of planets that were announced and then withdrawn. The Forgotten Men have bald heads and plain matte gray-beige skin, plain gray coats and very neat shoes, and their faces slip out of memory as soon as they leave with a copy of your manifest.

Fomalhaut has a bright dust ring of crushed rock, and a planet photographed there later turned out to be a cloud from a collision. The Junkwrights are squat, two-armed crews who buy broken cargo at half price and say that dust cloud was them, taking something apart.

Bisola

The name means “two suns.”

Home world Kepler-47, a planet system with two suns

Dihelius geminus (proposed)

Kepler-47 was the first system found with more than one planet circling two suns, so the sky there shows a golden sun and a small red one. Bisola look almost human, with rows of blue dots on the cheeks and gold pendants hung with stones. When both suns go down, their cheek dots glow soft blue: the nightfall glow. Every bill they pay is two different goods.

The Hushed

Home world Epsilon Eridani

Susurrator eridani (proposed)

Epsilon Eridani is a young, stormy star about 10.5 light-years away, and in 1960 it was one of the first stars anyone pointed a radio dish at. The Hushed are hooded, with deep purple skin, pale almond eyes and no mouth or large ears. They communicate mind to mind.

Mimics

Home world Luyten's Star

Echomorphus luyteni (proposed)

In 2017 a radio dish in Norway beamed music and a short lesson toward a planet at Luyten's Star, about 12 light-years away. Mimics are soft and putty-gray, never quite the same shape twice. Copying you is how they say hello, and their true eyes stay two small dark spots. The crew's lock-screen visitor Vex is a Mimic: it copied the crew's password, and only those two dark spots give it away.
